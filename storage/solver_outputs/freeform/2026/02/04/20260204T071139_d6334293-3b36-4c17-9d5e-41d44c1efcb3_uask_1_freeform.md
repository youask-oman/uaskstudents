# Recognized Problem
Find the asymptotes of the rational function $\dfrac{2x^2+3}{x-1}$.

# Domain Constraints
• $x \neq 1 $

# Steps
## Step 1: Perform polynomial division
Step 1: Polynomial division: Divide $ 2x^2+0x+3 $ by $ x-1 $ to write the function as a polynomial plus remainder, yielding $ 2x+2+\dfrac{5}{x-1}$.

## Step 2: Identify vertical asymptote
Step 2: Vertical asymptote test: Since the denominator is zero at $x=1 $ and the numerator is $ 2(1)^2+3=5\neq0 $, there is a vertical asymptote at $ x=1 $.

## Step 3: Identify oblique asymptote form
Step 3: Oblique asymptote extraction: The quotient from division $ 2x+2 $ gives the oblique (slant) asymptote $ y=2x+2 $ as $ x\to\pm\infty $ because the remainder term $\dfrac{5}{x-1}\to0$.

## Step 4: Confirm behavior at infinity
Step 4: End behavior check: As $ x\to\infty $ or $ x\to-\infty $, the function approaches $ 2x+2 $ since the remainder tends to zero, confirming the slant asymptote.

## Step 5: State no horizontal asymptote
Step 5: Horizontal asymptote absence: Because the degree of the numerator is one higher than the denominator, there is no horizontal asymptote, only the oblique one found.

## Step 6: Summarize asymptotes
Step 6: Final summary: The function has a vertical asymptote $ x=1 $ and an oblique asymptote $ y=2x+2$, with no other asymptotes.
