# Recognized Problem
Find the asymptotes of the rational function f(x) = (2x^2 + 3)/(x - 1).

# Domain Constraints
• Denominator: x - 1 ≠ 0  
• Combined conclusion: x ≠ 1

# Steps

## Step 1: Determine vertical asymptotes.
Step 1: Determine vertical asymptotes: Identify points where denominator equals zero and numerator is nonzero — x = 1 is a candidate vertical asymptote because x - 1 = 0 and numerator 2(1)^2 + 3 = 5 ≠ 0.

## Step 2: Decide on polynomial long division necessity.
Step 2: Decide on polynomial long division necessity: Recognize degree numerator (2) > degree denominator (1) so an oblique or linear asymptote may exist requiring division.

## Step 3: Perform polynomial long division setup.
Step 3: Perform polynomial long division setup: Divide 2x^2 + 0x + 3 by x - 1 by arranging terms in descending powers.

## Step 4: Compute leading term of quotient.
Step 4: Compute leading term of quotient: Divide 2x^2 by x to obtain 2x and write 2x as first quotient term.

## Step 5: Multiply and subtract first step.
Step 5: Multiply and subtract first step: Multiply (x - 1) by 2x to get 2x^2 - 2x and subtract from 2x^2 + 0x + 3 to obtain remainder 2x + 3.

## Step 6: Continue division for next quotient term.
Step 6: Continue division for next quotient term: Divide 2x by x to obtain +2 and append +2 to quotient.
