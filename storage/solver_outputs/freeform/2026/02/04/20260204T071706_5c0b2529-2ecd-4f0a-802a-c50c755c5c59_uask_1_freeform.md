# Recognized Problem
Find the asymptotes of the rational function $\frac{2x^2+3}{x-1}$.

# Domain Constraints
• $x \neq 1 $

# Steps
## Step 1: Perform polynomial division
Step 1: Divide polynomial: Divide $ 2x^2+0x+3 $ by $ x-1 $ to get quotient and remainder, yielding $ 2x+2+\frac{5}{x-1}$.

## Step 2: Identify vertical asymptote
Step 2: Vertical asymptote: Since the denominator is zero only at $x=1 $ and numerator is nonzero there, $ x=1 $ is a vertical asymptote.

## Step 3: Extract oblique asymptote
Step 3: Oblique asymptote equation: The quotient $ 2x+2 $ gives the oblique (slant) asymptote $ y=2x+2 $.

## Step 4: Check behavior near infinity
Step 4: Confirm leading behavior: As $ x\to\pm\infty $ the remainder term $\frac{5}{x-1}\to0$, so the graph approaches $ y=2x+2 $.

## Step 5: Check behavior near vertical asymptote sides
Step 5: One-sided limits at $ x=1 $: As $ x\to1^+$, $\frac{5}{x-1}\to+\infty $ so $ y\to+\infty $, and as $ x\to1^-$, $\frac{5}{x-1}\to-\infty $ so $ y\to-\infty $.

## Step 6: Summarize asymptotes
Step 6: List asymptotes concisely: Therefore the function has a vertical asymptote $ x=1 $ and an oblique asymptote $ y=2x+2$.
