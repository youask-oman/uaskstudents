To solve the problem \(\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x)\), we will use a fundamental trigonometric identity. Let's go through the steps in detail:

**Step 1: State the Pythagorean Trigonometric Identity**
The Pythagorean trigonometric identity states that for any angle \(x\),
\[
\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1.
\]

**Step 2: Apply the Pythagorean Trigonometric Identity**
Since \(\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x)\) is exactly the left-hand side of the identity, we can directly substitute the right-hand side:
\[
\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1.
\]

**Step 3: Verify the Identity**
To verify the identity, we can consider a specific value of \(x\). Let's choose \(x = 0\):
\[
\sin^2(0) + \cos^2(0) = 0^2 + 1^2 = 0 + 1 = 1.
\]
This confirms that the identity holds for \(x = 0\).

**Step 4: Verify with Another Value**
Let's choose another value, say \(x = \frac{\pi}{2}\):
\[
\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) + \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1^2 + 0^2 = 1 + 0 = 1.
\]
This confirms that the identity holds for \(x = \frac{\pi}{2}\).

**Step 5: Generalize the Result**
Since the Pythagorean trigonometric identity is true for all angles \(x\), we can conclude that:
\[
\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1.
\]
