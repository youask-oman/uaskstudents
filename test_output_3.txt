To find the derivative of the function \( f(x) = 3x^4 - 2x^2 + 5 \), we will use the power rule for differentiation. The power rule states that if \( f(x) = x^n \), then \( f'(x) = n x^{n-1} \).

Let's apply the power rule to each term in the function step by step.

**Step 1: Differentiate \( 3x^4 \)**
The derivative of \( x^4 \) is \( 4x^3 \). Therefore, the derivative of \( 3x^4 \) is:
\[ 3 \cdot 4x^3 = 12x^3 \]

**Step 2: Differentiate \( -2x^2 \)**
The derivative of \( x^2 \) is \( 2x \). Therefore, the derivative of \( -2x^2 \) is:
\[ -2 \cdot 2x = -4x \]

**Step 3: Differentiate \( 5 \)**
The derivative of a constant is \( 0 \). Therefore, the derivative of \( 5 \) is:
\[ 0 \]

**Step 4: Combine the derivatives**
Now, we combine the derivatives of each term to get the derivative of the entire function:
\[ f'(x) = 12x^3 - 4x + 0 = 12x^3 - 4x \]

So, the derivative of \( f(x) = 3x^4 - 2x^2 + 5 \) is:
\[ \boxed{12x^3 - 4x} \]
