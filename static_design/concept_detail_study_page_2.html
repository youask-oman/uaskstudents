Newtonian Mechanics: Second Law

The fundamental relationship between an object's mass, its acceleration, and the net force applied to it.

science Physics
school College Level
schedule 15 min study
bolt High Yield

Formal Definition

Newton's Second Law of Motion states that the acceleration of an object as produced by a net force is directly proportional to the magnitude of the net force, in the same direction as the net force, and inversely proportional to the mass of the object.

Intuitive Explanation

Think of it as the "effort" equation. If you want to push a heavy shopping cart (large m) at the same speed as a light one, you need to push much harder (larger F).

Key Takeaway

Acceleration depends on both force and mass. If you double the force, you double the acceleration. If you double the mass, you halve the acceleration. This inverse relationship is why heavy objects are harder to start (and stop) moving.
